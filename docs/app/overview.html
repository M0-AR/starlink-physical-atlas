What is up there — and how strongly do we know it?

One snapshot, one evidence chain. The 2026-11-23 catalog resolves into 11,198 objects, 11 altitude shells, five inclination families, and a claim ledger that states the strength of every headline number.

Headline findings

Five numbers that change the story.

These are not independent facts. They are the output of one frozen snapshot and one reproducible pipeline.

Population66.25%

Starlink accounts for 11,199 of 16,904 active catalog objects in the 2026-11-23 comparison. The numerator matches the GP snapshot.

Altitude465.5 km

The median is far below the old 550 km shorthand. 97.95% of objects are below 500 km; the maximum is 574.6 km.

Structure11 / 5

Eleven altitude shells across five inclination families. 92.20% of objects are assigned to a resolved shell; 873 remain unassigned.

Dynamics−97 km

The 70° family moved by about −97 km between April and November 2026. The 43° family stayed essentially flat.

Service geometry55 visible

A 25° elevation mask leaves a median of 55 visible satellites per site. The best-satellite handover interval is 30 seconds.

Evidence0 contradicted

Of 45 claims: 20 verified, 7 consistent, 5 partially verified, 13 external-only. The ledger has no contradicted or untested claims.
