The constellation is not one altitude.

What “missing” means

The Kaggle snapshots do not carry NORAD IDs, so cross-snapshot matching uses normalized Starlink names. A name that disappears from the later catalog is catalog attrition. It may have reentered, been renamed, or fallen outside the public extract.

The measured attrition rate is 0.46% per month for April → November 2026, or about 47 objects per month.

The 97.4° shell has a wide p10–p90 interval because it mixes a stable subgroup with a large descending cohort. The median alone is not the whole distribution.

The long transition

This earlier window captures the broad move away from the historical ~550 km operating band. The 53.1° family had 69.6% of matched objects within ±10 km, while the 43° family was still redistributing across shells.

Matched 6,661 of 7,153; 492 missing; 3,458 new. 0.59% monthly attrition.

April 2026 → November 2026

The settled structure

By late 2026, 94.1% of the 43° matched population is within ±10 km of its April altitude. The 70° and 97.4° families are the moving parts. That is the structure the atlas resolves.

Matched 9,761 of 10,119; 358 missing; 1,437 new. 0.46% monthly attrition.
