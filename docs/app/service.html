05 / Service geometry

What the geometry supports — and what it does not.

A ten-site, 24-hour simulation with a 25° elevation mask. The result is abundant visibility, frequent handover, measurable Doppler, and a radio-path latency bound that is not an end-to-end internet promise.

The map is illustrative: the sites are real coordinates, but the background is an equirectangular Earth texture. Dot size is constant; hover a dot for the site summary.

Visible satellites per site

Median number above a 25° elevation mask over 24 hours. The range is 43–65 across the ten sites.

Handover interval

Best-satellite model: the serving satellite changes when another offers a better elevation. This is a geometric lower bound on handover frequency, not an operational scheduler.

Doppler and radio RTT

Doppler p95 per site and the four-leg radio RTT bounds. The band is the gateway-at-nadir to gateway-at-horizon assumption.

What is measured

Geometry, not traffic.

Elevation, slant range, pass duration, Doppler, and the best-satellite handover. The model uses SGP4 positions at 15-second steps and a fixed 25° mask.

What is assumed

Two gateway extremes.

Public data has no gateway coordinates. The RTT bounds place the gateway at nadir and at the horizon, producing a transparent range rather than a false point estimate.

What is excluded

Everything operational.

Fiber, routing, processing, scheduling, weather, congestion, antenna tracking, and user-terminal effects are outside the model. The 6.19–6.27 ms number is radio only.
